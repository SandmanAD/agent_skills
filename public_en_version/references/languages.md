# Language specifics

Read the section for your language before writing cards. General rules are in SKILL.md; this file covers only what differs between languages.

Hints go into the translation field, so they are written in the translation language the user chose. The examples below use English as the translation language; for another language use its usual dictionary abbreviations. Common English ones: m., f., n. (gender), pl. (plural), anim./inanim. (animate/inanimate), impf./pf. (imperfective/perfective aspect), + acc./+ dat./… (case government), colloq., rude, literary, poet.

A hint is short and only where it is easy to go wrong without it: irregular forms, non-obvious gender, special government. Do not spell out regular forms.

## English

**Norm:** the one the user chose before; otherwise General American (say so in one sentence). British — the modern standard (SSBE/RP).

**First field:** nouns without an article; verbs without *to*; phrasal verbs and collocations whole (*give up*, *make a decision*).

**Hints** (written in the translation language; shown here in English for readability): irregular verbs — "to go (went, gone)"; irregular plural — "child (pl. children)"; uncountable — "advice (uncountable)".

**IPA:**
- GA: rhotic /ɹ/ or /r/ (pick one symbol), /ɝ/ and /ɚ/, /ɑ/ in *hot*, /oʊ/; do not show the flap [ɾ] in broad transcription.
- British: /ɒ/, /əʊ/, non-rhotic, length /iː uː ɑː ɔː ɜː/.
- Secondary stress ˌ in long words.

**Obsolete:** archaic, obsolete, dated (dated — careful, often still understood and used).

**Dictionaries:** Cambridge Dictionary (UK and US), Oxford Learner's Dictionaries, Longman (LDOCE). Merriam-Webster uses its own notation, not IPA.

## German

**Norm:** standard German pronunciation (Duden, Germany). Austrian/Swiss variants only on request.

**First field:**
- nouns with the definite article and a capital letter: *der Tisch*, *die Lampe*, *das Haus*;
- verbs in the infinitive, reflexives with *sich*: *sich freuen*.

**Hints:**
- plural — always, it is unpredictable: "table (pl. Tische)"; plural only: "parents (pl. only)";
- strong and irregular verbs: "to drive (fährt, fuhr, ist gefahren)";
- separable prefix: "to call (separable: ruft an)";
- government: "to wait for (auf + acc.)".

**IPA:**
- Long and short vowels: /iː eː ɛː aː oː uː yː øː/ vs /ɪ ɛ a ɔ ʊ ʏ œ/.
- Final devoicing: *Tag* /taːk/, *Hund* /hʊnt/.
- ich-Laut /ç/ after front vowels and consonants, ach-Laut /x/ after back vowels; *-ig* /ɪç/.
- r: /ʁ/ at the start of a syllable; vocalised /ɐ/ in *-er* and /ɐ̯/ after a long vowel (*Tier* /tiːɐ̯/). Choose one system and keep it.
- *z* /ts/; *st-*, *sp-* at the start of a stem /ʃt ʃp/.
- The glottal stop /ʔ/ before an initial vowel: either everywhere or nowhere.
- The article in its full form: *der Tisch* /deːɐ̯ ˈtɪʃ/, *das Haus* /das ˈhaʊ̯s/.

**Obsolete:** veraltet (skip), veraltend (usually fine, with a label).

**Dictionaries:** Duden online ("Aussprache" section), DWDS (IPA), Wiktionary (de).

## French

**Norm:** standard (metropolitan) French.

**First field:**
- nouns with the definite article: *la maison*, *le livre*;
- if the article is elided to *l'*, gender must go into the hint: "tree (m.)" for *l'arbre*;
- verbs in the infinitive, pronominal verbs with *se*: *se lever*.

**Hints:**
- the feminine of an adjective if it is not formed with a simple *-e*: "beautiful (f. belle; before a vowel bel)";
- irregular plural: "eye (pl. les yeux)";
- irregular verb conjugation — key forms only: "to go (je vais, j'irai, allé; être)".

**IPA:**
- No stress marks.
- Nasals /ɑ̃ ɔ̃ ɛ̃/. /œ̃/ is either distinguished everywhere or merged with /ɛ̃/ everywhere.
- Distinguish /e ɛ/, /o ɔ/, /ø œ/; /ə/; /ʁ/; semivowels /j w ɥ/ (*nuit* /nɥi/).
- Do not write silent final consonants.
- Show obligatory liaison inside the unit: *les amis* /lez‿ami/. *h aspiré* blocks liaison: *le héros* /lə eʁo/.

**Obsolete:** vx / vieux, vieilli (vieilli — careful), littér. (bookish, fine with a label).

**Dictionaries:** Le Robert en ligne (gives IPA), Wiktionnaire, Larousse (audio).

## Spanish

**Norm:** Castilian by default (Spain, distinción /θ/–/s/); seseo on a Latin American request. Name the chosen variant in one sentence.

**First field:**
- nouns with the article: *el libro*, *la mano*;
- non-obvious gender goes into the hint: *el agua* "water (f.)", *el problema* "problem (m.)";
- verbs in the infinitive, reflexives with *-se*: *levantarse*.

**Hints:**
- stem changes: "to think (ie: pienso)", "can, to be able (ue: puedo)";
- irregular verbs: "to have (tengo, tuve)";
- for *ser* / *estar* — the meaning in the translation.

**IPA:**
- Stress ˈ in every polysyllabic word, even without a written accent.
- Broad /b d ɡ/ (allophones [β ð ɣ] only if you chose narrow transcription for the whole deck).
- /ɾ/ vs /r/ (*pero* / *perro*); *j*, *ge/gi* — /x/; *y/ll* — /ʝ/ (yeísmo); *h* silent; *ñ* /ɲ/; *ch* /tʃ/.
- Castilian: *z*, *ce/ci* — /θ/.

**Obsolete:** DLE marks desus. (skip) and p. us. (poco usado — rarely used, usually skip).

**Dictionaries:** DLE (RAE) — meanings and labels; Wiktionary for IPA; Diccionario panhispánico de dudas — for disputed cases.

## Italian

**Norm:** standard Italian pronunciation (DOP).

**First field:**
- nouns with the definite article: *il libro*, *lo zaino*, *la casa*;
- with *l'*, gender goes into the hint: "friend (m.)" for *l'amico*;
- verbs in the infinitive, reflexives with *-si*: *alzarsi*.

**Hints:**
- irregular plural: "egg (pl. le uova)", "man (pl. gli uomini)";
- the auxiliary if it is *essere*: "to go (aux. essere; vado)".

**IPA:**
- Distinguish /e ɛ/ and /o ɔ/ under stress (unstressed only /e o/); /ts dz/, /tʃ dʒ/, /ʃ/.
- Write double consonants with two symbols (/tt/) or a length mark (/tː/) — consistently.
- /ɲ ʎ ʃ ts dz/ between vowels are always long: /ɲɲ ʎʎ ʃʃ/.
- Length of a stressed vowel in an open syllable (*cane* /ˈkaːne/): either mark it everywhere or nowhere.
- Always mark stress, especially in homographs: *àncora* / *ancóra*.
- Raddoppiamento fonosintattico between words may be left out in broad transcription — say so once in a note on the file, not on every card.

**Obsolete:** ant. / antico (skip), lett. / letterario (poetic/bookish, take case by case). Poetic truncations in songs (*cor*, *amor*) — reduce to the standard lemma.

**Dictionaries:** DOP (dizionario.rai.it), DiPI (Canepari), Treccani, Garzanti.

## Portuguese

**Norm:** ask if unclear. With no choice — Brazilian, and say so; European on request. Do not mix.

**First field:** nouns with the article (*o livro*, *a casa*); verbs in the infinitive.

**IPA:**
- Nasal vowels and diphthongs (/ɐ̃w̃/ in *-ão*).
- Brazilian: /tʃ dʒ/ before /i/ (*tia*, *dia*).
- European: strong reduction of unstressed vowels, /ɨ/.
- /ʁ/ vs /ɾ/; /ʎ ɲ/.

**Dictionaries:** Infopédia (European, IPA), Priberam, Aulete (Brazilian), Wiktionary.

## Czech

**Norm:** spisovná výslovnost (standard pronunciation).

**First field:** nouns in the nominative singular (no articles); verbs in the infinitive; reflexives with *se/si*: *bát se*.

**Hints:**
- gender always, with animacy for masculines: "table (m. inanim.)", "book (f.)";
- irregular forms: "child (pl. děti)", "person (pl. lidé)";
- aspect: one aspect form per note, the pair in the hint: "to do (impf.; pf. udělat)";
- government: "to be afraid of (+ gen.)".

**False friends — mark them when translating into a Slavic language, especially Russian:**
- *čerstvý* — fresh (Russian *чёрствый* means "stale");
- *zápach* — stench, bad smell (Russian *запах* is any smell);
- *pozor* — attention (Russian *позор* means "disgrace");
- *úžasný* — amazing (Russian *ужасный* means "terrible");
- *vonět* — to smell nice (Russian *вонять* means "to stink").

**IPA:**
- Stress on the first syllable; put ˈ in every polysyllabic word for consistency. A monosyllabic preposition takes the stress: *do školy* /ˈdo ʃkolɪ/.
- Length is distinctive: *á* /aː/, *é* /ɛː/, *í ý* /iː/, *ó* /oː/, *ú ů* /uː/; short *i y* /ɪ/; *ou* /ou̯/.
- *ř* /r̝/; *ch* /x/; *h* /ɦ/; *c* /ts/; *č* /tʃ/; *š* /ʃ/; *ž* /ʒ/.
- *ě*: *dě tě ně* /ɟɛ cɛ ɲɛ/, *bě pě vě fě* /bjɛ pjɛ vjɛ fjɛ/, *mě* /mɲɛ/. *di ti ni* in native words — /ɟɪ cɪ ɲɪ/.
- Syllabic /r̩ l̩/: *krk* /kr̩k/, *vlk* /vl̩k/.
- Final devoicing (*led* /lɛt/) and voicing assimilation (*kde* /ɡdɛ/, *vstát* /fstaːt/).
- The glottal stop /ʔ/: either everywhere or nowhere.

**Obsolete:** zast. / zastaralé (skip), kniž. (bookish, fine with a label), expr., hovor. (colloquial).

**Dictionaries:** Internetová jazyková příručka (prirucka.ujc.cas.cz), SSJČ / SSČ (ssjc.ujc.cas.cz), Wiktionary.

## Polish

**Norm:** standard Polish pronunciation.

**First field:** nouns in the nominative singular; verbs in the infinitive; reflexives with *się*.

**Hints:**
- gender: "shop (m.)";
- irregular forms: "person (pl. ludzie)", "year (pl. lata)";
- aspect: "to do (impf.; pf. zrobić)"; government.

**False friends — mark them when translating into Russian:**
- *uroda* — beauty (Russian *урод* means "freak");
- *sklep* — shop (Russian *склеп* means "crypt");
- *dywan* — carpet (Russian *диван* means "sofa");
- *czas* — time (Russian *час* means "hour").

**IPA:**
- Stress on the penultimate syllable. Exceptions: *-yka/-ika* (*matematyka* /matɛˈmatɨka/), forms in *-liśmy/-łyśmy* — stress on the third syllable from the end.
- *sz* /ʂ/, *cz* /t͡ʂ/, *rz ż* /ʐ/, *dż* /d͡ʐ/, *ś* /ɕ/, *ć* /t͡ɕ/, *ź* /ʑ/, *dź* /d͡ʑ/, *ń* /ɲ/, *ł* /w/, *y* /ɨ/, *ó u* /u/.
- *ą ę*: nasal before fricatives, /ɔm ɔn ɛm ɛn/ before stops; final *ę* is usually /ɛ/. Keep one system.
- Tie bars in affricates (/t͡ʂ/): either everywhere or nowhere.
- Final devoicing and assimilation: *prz* /pʂ/, *trz* /tʂ/.

**Obsolete:** przest. (skip), daw. (skip), książk. (bookish, fine with a label).

**Dictionaries:** WSJP (wsjp.pl), SJP PWN (sjp.pwn.pl), Wiktionary.

## Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic

For these languages agree the Transcription field format with the user. If it is not IPA, run the script with `--transcription-format free`.

**Chinese (Mandarin):**
- First field — characters (simplified, unless the user chose traditional).
- Transcription — pinyin with tone marks (*nǐ hǎo*); IPA only on request.
- The measure word goes into the hint: "book (MW 本)".

**Japanese:**
- First field — the usual spelling (kanji or kana, as the word is normally written).
- Transcription — the reading in kana; romaji on request.
- Pitch accent on request (OJAD, NHK accent dictionary).

**Korean:**
- First field — hangul.
- Transcription — IPA or Revised Romanization, agree with the user.
- Account for sound changes: *합니다* /hamnida/.

**Arabic:**
- Ask: Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) or a dialect.
- Write the first field with vowel marks (harakat) for the learner.
- IPA is fine.

## Any other language

1. Determine the standard pronunciation norm; if there are several, name the chosen one.
2. The first field is the dictionary form by that language's lexicographic tradition. Give gender, plural, verb classes and aspect in the hint where they are unpredictable.
3. IPA in broad phonemic notation modelled on major dictionaries and Wiktionary. Mark stress if it is lexical.
4. Find out which labels that language's dictionaries use for obsolete and rare words, and apply the selection rule from SKILL.md.
5. Look for and mark false friends between the target language and the translation language.
